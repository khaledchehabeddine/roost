# Two-Key Identity

**Status:** Accepted

Identity is split into a permanent identity key (IK) and a rotatable discovery key (DK) because conflating identity with discovery would force a choice between constant identity rotation, which breaks trust continuity, and permanent prekey-bundle exposure, which limits forward secrecy. The IK carries the cryptographic identity that safety numbers and sender certificates bind to, while the IK-signed DK drives prekey-bundle freshness and serves as the X3DH identity key for the current discovery epoch. DK rotation produces a fresh DK and fresh prekey bundle without touching the IK, so a peer rotating their DK does not invalidate existing trust or in-flight safety numbers.

The first implementation keeps the permanent and rotatable concerns decoupled at the storage boundary. `load_identity` returns only the IK and does not verify the DK; DK integrity is checked when the DK is read (`current_discovery_key_bundle`) instead. This avoids a recovery deadlock: a corrupt or absent DK never blocks loading the IK, and `rotate_discovery_key_now` self-heals it because minting and signing a fresh DK needs only the IK. The DK carries a signed monotonic `u32` epoch, but the DK is not part of the encrypted identity backup, so restoring an Identity onto a new Device starts a fresh epoch lineage at 0; peers judge freshness from the DK's `created_at` and the IK signature, not epoch magnitude. (The signed pre-key's own verification key follows the DK: under the two-key identity it is the DK, not the IK — the X3DH-lite handshake is migrated to consume this with the full-X3DH work.)
